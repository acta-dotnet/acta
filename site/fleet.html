Cluster-wide

Add as many workers as you like. They share the work through your database.

Every worker is a peer: the same process loop, talking to the same rows. There is no control plane to run, no broker to stand up, and nothing to elect. Add a process and it starts claiming. Kill one and the others carry on, and the work it held comes back through a recovery pass any of them can run.

The claim is the election

sys.retention, sys.alerts and sys.outbox are recurring slots like any schedule: each fires once per cadence, run by whichever worker claims it. Crash recovery rides the heartbeat instead, so it cannot get stuck behind the crash it repairs. No worker is special, so there is nothing to fail over.

One setting, three windows

The heartbeat interval is the only coordination knob. The lease window is four heartbeats and the dead-worker threshold seven, both read-only, so replicas that agree on the heartbeat agree on everything else by construction.

Measured, under chaos

The rc.5 certification ran 24 worker processes of 64 executors each, 1,536 concurrent slots on one database, with a worker killed every five seconds. Every one of a million jobs finished and no recorded step ran twice, checked afterwards from the database's own ledger, on PostgreSQL and on SQL Server. What one run showed, not a capacity claim.
