Acta.Acta: durable background jobs for .NET

acta, n. pl. · Latin: the things that have been done; the official record of proceedings.

Durable background jobs for .NET.

Everything is a job, and every job is a row.

Tasks, recurring syncs, parents with children, and long waits run through one engine, on the Postgres, SQL Server, or SQLite you already run. When a worker dies mid-job, the next one continues from the committed state.

Get started How Acta works

What you get

Retries and crash recoveryA dead worker's jobs go to another worker, and retries follow a backoff you declare. Steps, sleeps, and signalsWait hours or days without holding a thread; a finished step returns its recorded result instead of running again. Recurring jobs on a scheduleA cron or an interval fires one permanent job again and again; it keeps its state between firings. Order, concurrency, and rate limitsLanes keep one customer's jobs in order, keys and limits bound how many run, rates pace how often. Many workers, no coordinatorAdd a worker and it starts claiming; stop one and its work goes to the others.

The flagship

A job that runs every five seconds, remembers how far it got, and survives losing its worker.

One job keeps a local copy of a web shop's orders current: each firing reads where the last one stopped, applies the next batch, and writes the new position back.

C# · the handler, simplified from the lab
[Job("sync-orders")]
[Schedule("every-5s", "PT5S")]
public async Task Sync(JobContext ctx, CancellationToken ct)
{
    // durable job state: it outlives the firing, the retry, and the worker
    var watermark = await ctx.GetVariableOrDefaultAsync("watermark", 0L, ct);
    var changes = await shop.ChangesAfterAsync(watermark, 40, ct);
    if (changes.Count == 0) return;   // up to date: nothing to do until the next firing
    await store.ApplyAsync(changes, ct);   // an upsert: a replay lands once
    await ctx.SetVariableAsync("watermark", changes[^1].ChangeId, ct);
}

Stop the worker mid-batch, with a deploy or a kill, and the next one picks the job up at the same watermark; a replayed batch is an upsert, so it lands once.
