Inside a worker

How one worker claims, runs, and finalizes a job.

Three agents and one table. The claimer takes rows from the database, the executors run handlers, the finalizer writes what happened, and an in-flight registry holds every claim from the moment it is taken until its completion has committed. The heartbeat renews exactly what the registry holds, so a row nothing holds runs out its lease and goes back to recovery.

What each stage decides

What each stage decides.

Claimer
Waits for one permit, then takes every other free one, and claims that many rows in one call, each moved straight to Executing. After a claim that found work, it first lets the executors take those rows and collects every permit they free, so the next claim asks for all of them instead of the one or two free at that instant. Unused permits go back at once. An empty claim sleeps until the next row is due, at least 50 ms and at most 1 s, jittered up to 100 ms, and a wakeup ends the sleep early. A failed claim backs off a full second.
The recovery slot
When a claim returns the namespace's sys.recovery row, it runs on its own task without a permit, so a worker whose executors are all busy still sweeps. A slot monitor also checks it every seven minutes.
Executor
Skips an entry whose deadline already fired. Then admission (concurrency slot, rate meter), the handler, and the completion record. It hands the entry to the finalizer and only then returns its permit.
Finalizer
One per worker. Reads one completion, then everything already waiting, up to 128 completions or 4 MiB of results; if that first read found a backlog, it waits up to 50 ms more to fill the batch, and a lone completion never waits. They all go into one complete_executions call, whatever their shape: a success or failure, a retry, a signal wait, a recurring fire, a child telling its parent, a lane handing on. The write repeats until it lands; then the wakes, the metric, and the entry is removed.
Heartbeat
Every 45 s it snapshots the registry and makes one call with the held (job, execution) pairs and lock holds. A renewed row re-arms its entry's deadline. A row locked by another transaction keeps its deadline for the next beat. A row not answered cancels a Claimed or Executing entry, while a Finalizing entry is left to its write. A lock not renewed cancels its attempt. When the namespace's newest definition change moved, it reloads the policy.

Every deadline comes from one setting: the heartbeat interval.

A row's status

A job's status, and its entry in the worker.

In the ledger

Ready → Executing → Succeeded | Failed | Ready

The claim itself starts the execution, so there is no separate start write. A claim whose answer was lost is treated as a crash right after it.

In the registry

Claimed → Executing → Finalizing → removed

Reported by acta.jobs.inflight, tagged by state; batch sizes by acta.completions.batch.
