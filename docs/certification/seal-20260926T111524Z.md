participant processes, two namespaces, one shared run id, on PostgreSQL. This is the only gate where

the workers holding its turns are being killed. The harness ran as a Native AOT executable.

## The rate contract under chaos

`metered` is a job whose body writes one witness note and returns; its only policy is
`RateLimit = "600/m"`, ten admissions a second with a burst of ten. The contract is the upper
envelope: in any window of T seconds the meter admits at most `R*(T + W) + B` handler starts, with W
the one-second validity of a booked turn, so 30 in any second and 120 in any ten seconds. The check
counts the witness notes, one per admitted attempt, over every window an admission opens. The
busiest second held 20 and the busiest ten seconds 110, with three participants killing the workers
that held those turns.

**The 49 Failed rows are the point of this gate, not a blemish on it.** This is the only run that
interrupts charges mid-body, so a job whose retry budget is spent while its charge is in flight lands
terminal Failed by design. `expected-outcome` compares every terminal status against what the
workload declared it should be and returned 0, so all 49 are inside the declared envelope. A run of
this shape with zero failures would mean the interruption never landed.

## Scope

Two namespaces, three participants, one machine, one PostgreSQL schema. Checks are
`anvil/Anvil/certify.sql` bound to `certv1dens` plus the `rate-contract` window scan in
`CertifyVerdict`; re-run `anvil certify` against that schema to reproduce the verdict.
`at-most-once` and `namespace-isolation` are the two properties this gate exists to make
non-vacuous; rolling deploys are covered by the `runtime.rolling-deploy` conformance spec on all
three providers, not by this gate.
