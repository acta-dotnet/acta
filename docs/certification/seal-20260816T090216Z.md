## Namespace isolation, falsifiable and held again

`cert-b` was not an empty row: it registered 77 workers, ran its recurring system slots plus
Anvil's pulse, and recorded its own execution events (26 of them, beside `cert-a`'s 14,026), all
while every participant killed workers every five seconds. The check compares each execution
event's namespace against its owning job's across the whole schema: **zero rows**. No worker in
either namespace ever emitted an event for work it did not own, under contention that included a
second live namespace to get wrong.

The three participants behaved as the shape requires, which is itself part of the evidence. `p2`
joined the seeder's run twenty-nine seconds after it started, claimed from the same queue for the
whole chaos window, and exited cleanly when the run's work went terminal — four seconds before the
seeder sealed, having correctly declined to render a verdict it does not own. `p3` reported `done
0` at every sample by design: it holds no seeded workload, only `cert-b`'s five recurring slots,
and it was stopped after the seeder's verdict.

## The double-spend claim, non-vacuous this time

The three single-participant runs of this round all reported `at-most-once = 0` with zero
interrupted charges — vacuously true. Here the kills landed: of 400 charges, **44 were interrupted
mid-body** and terminalized as ambiguous rather than re-run, 356 succeeded, and every one of the
400 wrote exactly one witness note — **no charge body ever ran twice** (maximum bodies per job: 1).
That is the contract stated exactly: zero or one executions, never two, with an honest "this may
have happened once" instead of a confident second charge.
