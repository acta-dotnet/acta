# Acta certification seal

**Verdict: PASS** — **1,000,000 jobs**, all eight checks green, zero failures. The first
million-job seal, and the first at production concurrency: 24 worker processes, 1,536 concurrent
executor slots, 155 of those processes killed outright while they held work.

Every one of the 9,363 interrupted jobs was reclaimed and finished on its **second** attempt. Not
one needed a third, and none exhausted its retry budget. The highest `execution_number` anywhere in

## The at-least-once evidence, counted rather than asserted

Each step body writes a note before doing its work, so a body that ran twice leaves two notes while
the `steps` table — one row per `(job, step_name)`, updated in place — keeps only the current state.

**5,000,000 step rows. 5,008,420 step-body notes.**

So **8,420 step bodies ran a second time**, which is exactly what at-least-once means and exactly
what Acta documents. What did *not* happen, on any of them, is the thing that would matter:
`step-replay` is 0, meaning no step row was ever recorded twice and no note was ever timestamped
after its own step's success. The difference between "the body re-ran" and "the work was recorded
twice" is the whole product, and here it is as two numbers eight thousand apart.

The 8,420 re-run bodies sit just under the 9,363 orphaned attempts, which is the expected direction:
a worker killed before its body reached the note leaves an orphaned attempt with no second note.

## Reading the chaos numbers

Unlike the 10,000-job seals, these counts are not arithmetic. Those runs killed a fixed cadence
against a fixed slot count, so 72 kills × 8 slots = 576 exactly. Here the kill lands on whichever
worker is chosen, holding whatever it happens to hold, against a moving population of 24 processes
being continuously killed and respawned: 155 workers reclaimed dead, 9,363 slots orphaned, an
average of ~60 in-flight jobs lost per killed worker against a ceiling of 64. That the average sits
just under the ceiling is itself the finding — the workers were saturated when they died.

## Throughput

Sustained **267 jobs/s** across the run, against a theoretical ceiling of 307/s (1,536 slots ÷ 5s
per job). The rate was the same during the chaos window as after it, which says the run was
concurrency-bound rather than chaos-bound: killing a quarter of the fleet every 20 seconds did not
measurably slow the ledger down.

Connection pooling had to be bounded to get here. Left at Npgsql's default the 24 workers grew to
492 of PostgreSQL's 500 connections while only ~40 were doing work at any instant — sleeping jobs
hold slots, not sockets. Capping the pool at 16 per worker put the total at 384 with 120 to spare
and changed nothing else about the run.

## Scope

Single namespace, single machine, one provider. Multi-namespace ensemble and tenant spread remain
unrun; those are the rest of the W2 gate. The checks are `anvil/Anvil/certify.sql` with the schema
token bound to this run's schema; re-run them against the schema to reproduce the verdict.
